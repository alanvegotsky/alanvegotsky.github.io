Why the words

In the 1990s, Japanese researcher Dr. Masaru Emoto froze water and photographed the crystals. He reported that water exposed to kind words, prayer and gentle music formed beautiful, symmetrical shapes, while water met with harsh words formed broken, chaotic ones.

Science has never confirmed his results, so take them as poetry rather than proof. But the image is a good one to carry: your body is mostly water. If words can shape water, then the ones you soak in, including the ones you say to yourself, are worth choosing kindly. That is the whole idea behind this page.

Curious? Emoto's original crystal photos are on his site.

The words of Ken Vegotsky

Some of the affirmations in the player above come from Ken Vegotsky, a Toronto author who spent his life writing about the power of a positive frame of mind. After a near-fatal accident, he rebuilt his life on the belief that the words we choose shape the lives we lead. These are a few of his.

“Nearly dying has a way of bringing you back to life.”

“No longer can I stand by and watch life pass me by. My struggles helped me realize life is a rare and beautiful gift.”

“People and their love, support and friendship are the true treasures in life.”

“The powers of self-love and self-discipline are awesome.”

“I wake up in the morning and this start of the new day is miraculous enough for me.”

“Laughter is a wonderful medicine that heals the mind-body-soul.”

From The Ultimate Power: How to Unlock Your Mind-Body-Soul Potential (AGES Publications, 1995). Ken also wrote For Lovers Only and the No B.S. Self-Publishing Special Report. His books are free to borrow on the Internet Archive.

Good news from around the world

A positive frame of mind is easier to keep when you remember what is already going right. These are true stories, gathered from around the world. Come back now and then; the list changes as the world does.

The blue whales are coming back

Antarctic blue whales were hunted from hundreds of thousands down to just 418 animals. After fifty years of protection, researchers count more than 4,000 again, and the population is growing every year.

University of Washington News, November 2026

A secret mob that fights hate with kindness

When a woman's post was flooded with hateful comments, half a million strangers quietly organized to drown the trolls out with encouragement. The Kindness Mob has rescued about 400 bullied strangers this way, and tries to never let them know who sent the wave.

CBS News, October 2026

Sea turtles off the endangered list

Green sea turtles, once hunted heavily across the oceans, have recovered so well that they were moved from "endangered" to "least concern". On Aldabra atoll in the Seychelles, nesting has nearly tripled since 1980.

Smithsonian Magazine, October 2026

A four-year-old who waved at everyone

Roman, four years old in Concord, North Carolina, greeted every neighbor with a wave and a "hey". One by one they started stopping to talk, coming to his games and his birthday party, until a street of strangers had become a village.

CBS News, July 2026

"Dad, I think I've found my people"

Adam, a 23-year-old autistic man from High Wycombe, went to his first music festival alone. A group of strangers invited him to camp with them and looked out for him all five days. He has since been booked for his first DJ gigs.

BBC News, August 2026

She knocked on her loud neighbor's door

Instead of complaining about the noise upstairs, she checked on the man making it. He was living with Parkinson's and needed help. When she shared his story, strangers raised more than $100,000 for his care.

Good News Network, November 2026
